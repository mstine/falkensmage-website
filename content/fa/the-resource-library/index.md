The Foundations are the on-ramp — you read them once, in order, and they teach you the worldview, the grammar, and the practices. This is the other thing: the **reference shelf** you don't read straight through, you *consult.* When you forget what *albedo* means, or you want the one tarot book worth owning, or a snake showed up in a dream and you need somewhere to *start* — you come here.

Four shelves:

- **[The Book Shelf](/fa/the-book-shelf/)** — what to actually read, by area, with a plain word on why each one earns the spot. The real teachers, the canonical texts, the ones I'd hand you.
- **[The Jungian Glossary](/fa/the-jungian-glossary/)** — the vocabulary of the whole place, defined in plain language instead of textbook fog. *Shadow, anima, individuation, nigredo* — what they actually mean and where each gets taught.
- **[The Archetypes](/fa/the-archetypes/)** — the cast. A quick-reference companion to [Foundation II](/fa/archetype/): the major figures, their essence, their shadow form, and how to find your own stack.
- **[Dream-Motifs](/fa/dream-motifs-the-anti-dictionary/)** — the **anti-dictionary.** Not "snake means X." The archetypal territory a motif tends to point at, plus the question that hands you back to your own associations.

## The one rule of this whole shelf

It's a **launch pad, not an oracle.** Every reference here is a *starting point* — a direction to begin associating from, a door to walk through — never a final answer. The moment you treat the glossary as the truth, the book list as the only books, or the dream-motifs as a lookup table, you've turned a launch pad into a cage and missed the entire point.

The work is *yours* to do. These are just the tools I'd want on the shelf if I were starting over. Use them, argue with them, outgrow them.

Stay feral, folks.
